# 🔑 Stage 5: Move-in. What the data says, and what I'd do about it

Each finding follows the same chain: **What I saw → Why I think so → So what.**
Each recommendation follows: **Because… → I recommend… → I'd measure it by…**

## Key findings

### 1. Deliveries from Lagos to Lapai are 8 hours faster (26.0 h → 18.0 h)
- **What I saw:** average inbound transit fell by about a third.
- **Why I think so:** the carriers differ a lot, with SwiftRoute at 17.4 h, NorthLink at 22.9 h and Lagos Park Parcel at 27.9 h. Shipments timed to production days went from 0% to 100% in the two windows.
- **So what:** the gain came from choosing the right carrier and planning shipments, not from luck.

### 2. More orders arrive on time (64% → 82%, +18 percentage points)
- **What I saw:** 7 of 11 orders on time in the baseline window, 9 of 11 now.
- **Why I think so:** waiting for materials fell from about 52 h to about 21 h. In the baseline window that wait was about two-thirds (52 of 78 h) of the time orders spent waiting before printing started.
- **So what:** the biggest lever was the supply side, not the print shop. Fixing it is what moved on-time delivery.

### 3. Delivering an order costs 5% less (₦5,244 → ₦4,981)
- **What I saw:** cost per order fell by about ₦262.
- **Why I think so:** I measured cost *per order*, so growth in order numbers cannot hide the saving or fake one. Planned trips on faster carriers cost less than ad hoc ones.
- **So what:** better planning pays for itself.

### 4. Suppliers deliver more of what we ask for (73.6% → 96.6%)
- **What I saw:** about 97 of every 100 items requested now arrive, up from about 74.
- **Why I think so:** the share of purchase orders placed with vetted, trusted suppliers went from 25% to 100%.
- **So what:** vetting suppliers closes the supply gap.

## Recommendations

| # | Because… | I recommend… | I'd measure it by… |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the fastest carrier averaged 17.4 h against 27.9 h | keeping that carrier and timing shipments to production days | average transit hours each month |
| 2 | trusted suppliers fill about 97% of requests | vetting every new supplier before large orders | fill rate by supplier (the supplier scorecard) |
| 3 | in the latest window the only late reasons left were print backlog and customer changes | aiming the next gains at the print schedule and clearer customer briefs | late orders by reason |
| 4 | single months have only a few orders and look jumpy | judging results over six-month periods | the same two windows every time |

## Limits (said openly)
- The data is **simulated**. It was built to model a plausible improvement, so the "why" statements describe relationships I designed into the data. Use them as a demonstration of method, not as proof about a real business.
- The compact sample has 28 orders, so single-quarter percentages rest on very few orders. The two six-month windows are the reliable comparison.
- On-time and cost trends look noisy month by month, which is why the dashboard compares windows and shows quarterly values with order counts.
